Inbound vs Outbound Prediction: Planning Capacity With Better Data

2026-08-05 7 min read Planning

Capacity planning breaks down when teams only forecast demand. In reality, workload and risk come from both sides of the warehouse: what is expected to arrive (inbound) and what is expected to ship (outbound). Predicting both inside the ERP helps you plan labor, space, and customer commitments.

Key Takeaways

  • Inbound prediction estimates future receipts from purchase orders and supplier performance.
  • Outbound prediction estimates future shipments from sales orders and demand trend.
  • When combined, they help you plan constraints: labor, dock capacity, space, and cash.

Inbound prediction (what will arrive)

Inbound prediction answers: what will we receive, and when? It is driven by procurement and supplier execution:

  • Open purchase orders and promised dates.
  • Historical supplier lead time and lateness patterns.
  • Partial receipts and exception patterns.

Outbound prediction (what will ship)

Outbound prediction answers: what will we ship, and when? It is driven by customer demand and order flow:

  • Open sales orders and requested ship dates.
  • Shipment history and seasonality.
  • Backorders and allocation rules.

Why the combination matters

Inbound and outbound predictions expose the gaps you cannot see in a single report:

  • Space risk: inbound peaks colliding with slow outbound weeks.
  • Labor risk: receiving spikes and shipping spikes in the same week.
  • Cash risk: large inbound spend before outbound revenue.
  • Service risk: outbound commitments without inbound coverage.

Make prediction operational (not theoretical)

Predictions should trigger actions:

  • Reschedule receiving windows and prioritize urgent inbound.
  • Adjust reorder timing and purchase quantities.
  • Proactively communicate with customers when coverage is at risk.
  • Use audit trail history to understand why schedules changed.
