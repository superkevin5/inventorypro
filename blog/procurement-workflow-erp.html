Procurement Workflow in ERP: From Requisition to Purchase Order

2026-08-05 9 min read Procurement

Procurement is where supply chain control either starts or breaks. A clean procurement workflow in ERP gives you predictable buying, better supplier performance data, and fewer urgent purchases.

Key Takeaways

  • Use a consistent requisition → approval → PO → receiving flow, tied to inventory records.
  • Capture lead times, costs, and supplier performance from real transactions.
  • Use audit trail events to keep changes accountable without slowing teams down.

Step-by-step procurement workflow

1) Requisition (what you actually need)

A requisition is an internal request: SKU, quantity, target date, and reason. It is also a planning signal—when requisitions are late, inventory risk grows.

2) Approval (controls without bottlenecks)

Approvals should answer two questions: is the item valid, and is the spend justified? In ERP, approvals are traceable and consistent across teams.

3) Purchase order (commitment to the supplier)

A PO should capture price, lead time expectation, shipment terms, and any special requirements. The PO becomes the reference point for inbound prediction.

4) Receiving (turn commitments into reality)

Receipts should update inventory immediately and preserve traceability. If something arrives short or damaged, record it at receiving, not later in a spreadsheet.

5) Matching and exceptions (keep it simple)

You do not need to over-engineer. The key is to track exceptions: quantity mismatches, price discrepancies, and late deliveries.

What to capture for better reporting

  • Supplier lead time: expected vs actual receipt dates.
  • Cost components: purchase price, freight, duties, landed cost.
  • Change history: who edited quantities, dates, and prices (audit trail).

Common procurement problems (and what to fix first)

  • Rush POs: usually caused by missing reorder alerts or unreliable counts.
  • Supplier disputes: caused by missing PO/receipt traceability and unclear changes.
  • Inaccurate lead times: caused by not comparing expected vs actual receipt performance.
