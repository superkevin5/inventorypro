Inventory Forecasting in ERP: Reduce Stockouts and Excess Inventory

2026-08-05 8 min read Forecasting

Most forecasting problems are not math problems. They are workflow problems: sales, purchasing, and warehouse teams working from different spreadsheets with different assumptions. An ERP helps because forecast inputs (orders, shipments, lead times, receipts) come from real transactions.

Key Takeaways

  • Start with simple demand signals: historical shipments + open sales orders.
  • Convert demand into a replenishment plan using lead time and safety stock.
  • Use inbound and outbound predictions to see future workload and risk early.

Step 1: Choose demand signals you can trust

Use a short list of signals that are already recorded during normal operations:

  • Shipment history (what you actually delivered).
  • Sales orders (what customers are requesting).
  • Seasonality (if your business is cyclical, keep the same cycle definition).

Step 2: Add lead time reality

Two numbers drive most replenishment outcomes:

  • Supplier lead time: average and variability.
  • Internal handling time: receiving, putaway, inspection, picking capacity.

When lead time is inconsistent, an ERP makes it visible by comparing expected receipt dates to actual receipts.

Step 3: Set safety stock (simple and consistent)

Safety stock is a buffer against uncertainty. You do not need an overly complex formula to start; you need one that is applied consistently:

  • Higher safety stock for items with long lead times or volatile demand.
  • Lower safety stock for stable items or items with fast replenishment.

Step 4: Turn forecasts into actions

Forecasting only matters if it changes what you do next. In an ERP, forecasts should translate into:

  • Reorder alerts and proposed purchase quantities.
  • Planned inbound receipts to manage space and labor.
  • Planned outbound volume to manage picking and shipping capacity.

Inbound vs outbound prediction (why it matters)

Demand forecasting is only half the picture. Two predictions help you plan the next few weeks:

  • Inbound prediction: expected receipts and their timing (POs, shipments, supplier performance).
  • Outbound prediction: expected shipments based on sales orders and demand trend.
